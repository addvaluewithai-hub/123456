# Energy Research Lab Charter

## Mission

Discover, falsify, and develop simple-in-principle energy-conversion architectures that are physically defensible, quantitatively interesting, and capable of becoming a useful invention.

The lab is allowed to use real external energy sources or gradients. The interesting trick may control, gate, recycle, impedance-match, regenerate, or reshape energy flow, but every joule must have an identified source.

## Non-negotiables

1. **Inventors first, literature reviewers second.** Generate mechanisms, then use evidence to test them.
2. **No slogan physics.** Do not dismiss a concept with “conservation of energy”; identify the exact balance term, reset, state variable, or loss that closes the cycle.
3. **No hidden reset.** Count all control coordinates, restoration work, shunt motion, pumps, pressure sources, thermal regeneration, material-state changes, and startup/reset costs relevant to steady cycling.
4. **External input is allowed.** Output may exceed an explicit trigger/control pulse only when a real reservoir or gradient supplies the difference.
5. **No force-as-energy confusion.** Mechanical advantage or large force at small displacement is not energy gain.
6. **No untracked material depletion.** Demagnetization, sorbent depletion, chemical change, phase inventory, or pressure depletion is fuel unless restored in-cycle.
7. **Depth before breadth.** Do not kill a family from one toy model, one paper, or one device. Do not keep polishing a branch after its decisive unknown is hardware-only.
8. **Fundamental vs engineering.** Name which kind of limit is blocking progress.
9. **Evidence state must be explicit.** Distinguish analytical calculation, numerical simulation, published experiment, our bench measurement, and replication.
10. **Useful invention > exotic story.** New physics is not required; meaningful efficiency, power density, simplicity, controllability, cost, bandwidth, or recoverability improvements count.

## Verdict vocabulary

`HIGH PRIORITY`, `PROMISING`, `NEEDS DATA`, `LOW PRIORITY`, `REJECTED`, `SYNTHESIS`.

A verdict applies to the specific architecture/branch at its current scope. Generalize only when the argument actually generalizes.
